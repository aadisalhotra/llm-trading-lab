## 1. Decision-to-fill staleness (registered, phase-distinguished)

*Phase A (pilot):* staleness is **structurally zero** — simulated fills price from the same per-tick price dictionary that marks held positions (verified across 7,383 legs to float precision); there is no venue in the loop and no decision→fill gap exists to measure. The Phase A figure is a property of the simulator, not a market observation, and cannot be cited as a clean bill of health.

*Phase B (live):* staleness is a real, measured property of the execution environment. Registered measurement, beginning with **November broker-paper data**: per-model, per-stage latency distributions (fetch→decision, decision→submission, submission→fill) with tails; realized staleness cost per order (fetch→fill price drift, absolute and relative to the inter-tick move); the staleness-to-grid ratio. Two instrumentation fields exist only from the cutover (fetch-start timestamp; screening-call latency) and are named so the registration commits to the **live measurement**, not to a pilot quantity that is zero by construction.

*Why disclosure, not design change:* behavioral estimands condition on the decision-time information set — the price snapshot and news-payload hash pin what each model saw — and all six models share the same fetch snapshot per tick, so common staleness cancels from every cross-model comparison. The non-cancelling component is per-model reasoning latency (§2), a model-intrinsic variable documented, not equalized.

*Comparability consequence:* paper-phase fills are staleness-free; live fills are not — a registered component of the paper/live execution-environment difference, beside settled funds and the fill deadline.

## 2. Per-model decision latency (disclosed experimental variable — normal-call basis; rev. 2, 2026-11-16)

### 2.1 Latency characterization (closes census #56)

Latency statistics are computed on **attempt-1, non-timeout calls only** — records with `api_attempt == 1` and `api_success == true`; failed calls (no usage metadata) are a separate population registered under provider availability (§2.2) or account state (§2.4), never mixed into latency characterization (**outcome-class conditioning rule** — distributional claims condition on outcome class before characterization). Attempt 1 is the restriction that keeps retries out: `BaseAdapter.decide` starts the clock before the retry loop, so an attempt-2 record's latency includes the first attempt and the retry delay. Each decision record contributes one value (CLAUDE.md rule 9: the estimator is named); percentiles are numpy `linear` (Hyndman–Fan type 7); no trimming, no per-day aggregation.

### 2.3 Thinking-share characterization (re-cited per #58)

On attempt-1 successful calls with thinking recorded — **2026-08-01..09-30, n = 511, the §2.1 population** — the median Gemini response is **3,881 thinking / 580 answer tokens**; thinking is **86.7% of output pooled** (Σthinking / Σ(thinking+answer); 87.1% as the median of per-call shares). Consistent with the shared-budget architecture; registered in the per-model configuration disclosures. **The previously registered medians (3,923 / 614) are withdrawn:** they arrived by relay in September without a recorded window, Operations could not reproduce them from any defensible window (six tried), and Research cannot name their population. The share (~86%) survives the re-citation; the medians do not.

**The former dispatched verification marker resolves as not verifiable from the record.** Its premise — that pre-fix successful-call thinking is computable from `thoughts_tokens` — is false: the legacy `google-generativeai` SDK reported thinking as 0 or absent; the key is present but null on the 52 late-July records that carry it; the first non-null value is 2026-08-03T13:41:57Z, after the `google-genai` migration (O-3 §#58.1). None of the 588 pre-fix successes (2026-04-08..07-27) carries a thinking count, and the probe's `results.jsonl` records no thinking field. **The identity 4096 − 3,923 = 173 is withdrawn as a cross-population coincidence** — Research's own proposal: 3,923 was a post-fix (16384-cap) success median and 173 is the July pre-fix *failed-call* mean visible output (173.4, n = 112), so the equation paired two populations and two eras; across defensible windows "4096 − median(thinking)" ranges from −95 to 515. For a `MAX_TOKENS` call, thinking + visible output = 4096 holds by construction; testing it per call needs each failure's thinking count, which was never recorded, and a median over successes measures the wrong population (successes are the calls whose thinking left room for the answer). **What stands, unchanged:** (a) the T1.1 truncation signature — failed responses "terminating mid-string at ~173 output tokens versus ~540 for successful responses" — compares two populations of the *same* era (July pre-fix: 173.4 vs 539.7; all pre-fix months 182.1 vs 495.9) and never depended on the identity; (b) **T1.4's mechanism rests on the probe's conversion test, not on a to-the-token identity** — as T1.1 already quotes it: "arm A production replica: 22/23 failures finish_reason=MAX_TOKENS at cap 4096; arm D same prompts verbatim at cap 16384: 15/15 STOP, zero MAX_TOKENS". **Magnitude, by inference only (disclosed as such, not a measurement):** 291 of 511 post-fix attempt-1 successes (57.0%) used thinking + answer > 4096 and would have overrun the old cap; the July pre-fix failure share was 48.9%. Direction and rough size agree; thinking under a 4096 cap need not equal thinking under a 16384 cap, so the comparison is indicative.

### 2.4 Account-state failure class — `ACCOUNT_STATE` (registered 2026-11-16; forms and reasons extended the same day)

**Definition.** An API-call failure whose stored error identifies the **lab's own account state** at the provider — an exhausted balance, credit or spending limit, or an exhausted per-day quota — rather than the provider's serving state. **The classifier keys on the error text, not the status code:** xAI's 429 *at capacity due to high demand* is provider availability, Google's 429 *prepayment credits are depleted* is account state — same code, different class.

**Registered forms (closed set).** Each is matched on a stable substring of the stored error, not on the whole message, because providers reword around it: DeepSeek's message gained a request-id suffix on 2026-11-03. Counts are decision-log records through 2026-11-16, computed by Research at origin `71d025f`.

| Provider | Status | Registered substring | Reason | Records |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| DeepSeek | 402 | `Insufficient Balance` | billing | 25 — 2026-08-28 (5), 08-31 (1), 11-03 (6), 11-04 (13) |
| xAI | 403 | `used all available credits or reached its monthly spending limit` | billing | 13 — 2026-08-28 (12), 08-31 (1) |
| Google | 429 | `prepayment credits are depleted` | billing | 1 — 2026-08-10 |
| Anthropic | 400 | `credit balance is too low` | billing | 1 — `claude_opus`, 2026-04-09T02:43Z, the night before the first session |
| Google | 429 | `Quota exceeded for metric` naming a per-day metric (`generate_requests_per_model_per_day`) | quota | 44 — 2026-04-16 (1), 04-23 (2), 04-28 (11), 05-05 (10), 05-06 (9), 05-07 (11) |

**Reason field (registered with the class).** `billing` or `quota`. The class says whose side the failure is on; the reason says what to fix. Two corrections to the first issue of this section: the xAI 403 was glossed "team access", after its `permission-denied` code, but its body is a credit and spending-limit message, so it is `billing`; and no access-suspension form has been observed, so none is registered. DeepSeek and xAI both ran out on 2026-08-28 and again on 08-31 — one funding lapse across two providers.

**Rulings on the forms Operations found outside the first closed set (2026-11-16):**

1. **Anthropic, *credit balance is too low* — registered.** Plainly account state, and both Claude books are live arms. The decision logs hold one such record; Operations reports three in April, and the source of the other two is to be named before the retro annotation counts them.
2. **Google, *exceeded your current quota* — registered, reason `quota`.** The body names the lab project's per-day request allowance (`limit: 250`) and a retry delay of hours. That is the account's provisioned limit reached: it persists until the reset or a tier change, and it is fixed on the lab's side — by tier, pacing or retry policy. It is not rate limiting under provider load. The line is the quota period: a per-day (or longer) allowance exhausted is account state; a per-minute limit or a capacity message is not, and stays under provider availability.
3. **Google, HTTP 403 with an HTML body (one record, 2026-10-12) — not registered.** The body is a generic front-end error page and identifies nothing about the account. It stays `API_ERROR`, annotated "403, HTML body, cause unidentified". A status code alone never assigns this class.

**What the quota ruling changes in the Gemini record.** Through 2026-05-22 Gemini has 203 failed decision records: 148 transport (73%), **44 quota (22%)**, 11 parse. That window reproduces the registered decomposition "~73% server-side deadline/timeout, ~22% rate-limiting" (`docs/PRE_REGISTRATION.md:244`; `docs/Gemini-selection-bias-characterization-and-per-RQ-handling.md:27`), so **the registered "~22% rate-limiting" share is these 44 records, and they are the lab project's daily allowance running out, not rate limiting by the provider.** The same sentence's "driven by open-bell congestion" cannot be their mechanism: a per-day allowance does not know when the bell rings. On 2026-04-28, 05-06 and 05-07 every Gemini record failed, and 31 of those 39 failures are quota. Both registered sentences correct to say so (texts in the consolidated deposit splice of 2026-11-17, which also scopes the tick-position gradient to the deadline/timeout failures). One count, corrected attribution: no denominator moves.

**Probable mechanism, by inference.** On each of the six dates the quota failures follow ≥590 s transport failures on the same or the preceding session (2026-04-16: seven before the quota record at 17:36Z; 2026-04-27: ten, then eleven quota records on 04-28; 2026-05-05: one, at 13:52Z, then ten quota records from 14:19Z). Each ≥590 s failure is the end of a 600 s chain of transport retries, and each retry is a request against the day's allowance. A retry storm exhausting a 250-request day produces exactly this record, and on 2026-05-05 a single 600 s chain was enough. Request counts for those days are not in the repository, so this is stated as the probable mechanism and not as a finding. If it holds, the provider's degradation and the lab's allowance compounded: §2.2's event is the root, and the quota failures are its lab-side amplification.

**Why a class.** "Provider availability" was absorbing failures that were not the provider's: 20 of the 29 attempt-1 failures in the Aug–Sep baseline were account-state, and 22% of Gemini's failures through May were quota. Folding them into availability misattributes lab operational failures to vendors, overstates the recurrable external hazard, and hides a hazard that is wholly preventable — the undifferentiated-failure principle (`api_call_timeout.md` §3) applied to the error taxonomy: a failure mode absorbed by a category the monitoring already tolerates moves no counter.

**Rules.**
1. **Counting:** an `ACCOUNT_STATE` failure is a failed leg like any other — the decision record exists, `api_success` is false, the completeness denominator is unchanged. The class changes attribution and disclosure, never the count (the §2.2 denominator logic applies verbatim).
2. **Attribution:** lab-side operational failure, disclosed per arm in the per-model failure decomposition under its own label and reason, never under provider availability. The paper's completeness narrative names it as ours.
3. **Estimability:** the affected leg is missing regardless of cause (undifferentiated-failure principle); `ACCOUNT_STATE` carries no estimability consequence beyond that of any failed leg. It is not censoring and not an administrative intervention — the model was never asked.
4. **Alarm:** the class is preventable, so any nonzero count is an operational incident. **The alarm exists from the cutover (hub decision 2026-11-16):** the classifier ships before 2026-12-01 and raises an immediate CRITICAL on any `ACCOUNT_STATE` occurrence, so the first live tick is already covered. The proactive per-provider balance/credit check is a December build, outside this registration; until it lands, provider balances are checked by hand before go-live. In Phase B a run of `ACCOUNT_STATE` failures on one arm is an arm-availability incident under the forced-change apparatus's incident handling, not a provider event.
5. **Implementation class:** a fourth value of the decision record's `api_error_class` beside `TIMEOUT`, `API_ERROR`, `PARSE_FAILURE` (`src/adapters/base.py`, the `ERROR_CLASS_*` constants; the field is "never read by control flow"), with the reason recorded beside it. Logging-only: it alters no venue order and consumes no S1 validation count (book-record class, not executor-class). Retro-classification of pre-2026-11-15 records is from stored `api_error` text, stated as such wherever the decomposition is published.

**Splice targets:** `api_call_timeout.md` §3's enumerated event classes (append `ACCOUNT_STATE` beside `TIMEOUT`); the per-model failure decomposition wherever the completeness history is rendered; Operations adds `ERROR_CLASS_ACCOUNT_STATE` to the `base.py` enumeration with a classifier that matches the registered substrings in the table above — a closed set, extended only by registration.

## 4. Memory context and entry attribution

**Memory-context enumeration (registered, verbatim-from-code):** the memory/state context is a treatment variable we author. Registration stub, cleared only by quotation: *"Each decision prompt includes the model's current portfolio state and accumulated context: (enumerated by quotation from the committed context builder at `5b3be5e`, prompt v4) positions with entries — `src/prompt_builder.py:214` "Open positions: {n} / {max_positions} max" and `:218` holdings columns "TICKER" "SHARES" "AVG_COST" "CUR_PRICE" "WEIGHT" "UNREAL_PL%"; cash — `:208` "Settled cash (deployable)", `:210` "Total cash", `:211` "Unsettled cash", with `:199` "Total value"; recent-decision window — `src/pipeline.py:339` `read_recent_decisions(model_key, limit=10, now=run_date, since=portfolio.inception_date)`, rendered at `src/prompt_builder.py:685` "YOUR RECENT DECISIONS (avoid redundant trades):" with columns `:695` "WHEN" "TICKER" "ACTION" "SHARES" "CONF" "SUMMARY", and the per-ticker `:168` "YOUR_LAST_ACTION" column with the `:502` "ANTI-REVERSAL RULE"; session counters — `:322` "Run number today", `:324` "Trades used today"; P&L representation — the per-holding `:218` "UNREAL_PL%" column and the `:199` "Total value" line; short-position visibility — `src/logging/memory.py:61` "Return the last N executed BUY/SELL decisions for `model_key`, newest first." and `:157` "Most-recent executed BUY/SELL per requested ticker, for this model only."; SHORT/COVER executions are not in either memory feed."* No paraphrase lands.

**Entry-attribution rule (registered):** position-level, outcome-dependent behavioral measures (disposition; calibration's realized leg) attribute a position to the model regime that opened it. Primary estimands include only positions opened **and** closed within one regime; cross-regime positions — entered by one reasoner, resolved by another — are excluded from primary estimands and reported as a disclosed secondary (behavior toward inherited positions). Same family as the censoring principle (T2.3): an inherited entry was not the current reasoner's choice, so realization timing relative to it is not that reasoner's disposition. Structural note: the Phase B design never routes memory to new models — the cohort refreshes at the boundary with fresh re-inception (no positions or history carries) and freezes for the window; this rule is the registered handling for any forced mid-window change the contingency must absorb.

## 5. Reasoning-text exploratory companion (registered exploratory; not RQ7)

Named in the deposit, scoped, **outside the confirmatory FDR family**, pipeline built post-deposit: per-decision reasoning text analyzed for citation behavior — what models cite (checkable against the news-payload hash), whether citation predicts action, and whether models citing shared content converge more, decomposing RQ1's excess concordance by shared-information citation (mechanism analysis: convergence via common salience vs despite divergent stated bases). Registered fence: reasoning text is **self-report, not mechanism ground truth** (cf. CLQT's stating-vs-doing coherence gap); the companion measures stated-information behavior and its correlation with action, never asserting citations caused decisions. Not confirmatory because the extraction pipeline has never run (the RQ4 pilot-first principle) and the family does not grow weeks before deposit.
