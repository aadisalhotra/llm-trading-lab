## T1.1 — Forced-Change Contingency: Two Worked Examples

The forced-change contingency (§Forced-Change / Deprecation Exposure) is not hypothetical: two Phase A events exercised it end-to-end, under two different trigger types. Each example states the event, the boundary documentation, the segmentation handling, and the rule it instantiates.

### Worked example 1 — DeepSeek (provider-forced)

**Event.** The experiment launched on the `deepseek-reasoner` alias. On April 24, 2026, the provider silently repointed that alias to `deepseek-v4-flash` — a smaller, off-specification model — producing an undisclosed model-identity splice in the DeepSeek arm. Registered at `scripts/phase_a_integrity_ledger.json` → `integrity_events.deepseek_identity_splice`, which records `alias: "deepseek-reasoner"`, the transition `{"date": "2026-04-24", "to": "v4-flash", "note": "sub-frontier/off-spec, silent provider repoint"}`, `off_spec_window: "2026-04-24 to 2026-05-21"`, and `detection: "undetected ~4 weeks; month-boundary-only check"`. The splice was detected by investigation, not by the provider; the arm was re-pointed to `deepseek-v4-pro` at the config boundary `{"date": "2026-05-21", "to": "v4-pro"}` (same key, `transitions[1]`), with the dated pair recorded at `deepseek_identity_splice.first_success_note`: *"first_date 2026-05-21 is the config-repoint boundary; first logged v4-pro success is 2026-05-22 (n_success unchanged; flagged because the model-identity analysis turns on this boundary)."* The provider subsequently announced hard retirement of the `deepseek-reasoner` alias effective 2026-07-24 (migration completed ahead of the deadline), and launched the official V4 version in mid-July 2026. **The V4-GA boundary is the worked example's honest imperfection:** it arrived before the pre-launch identity capture was confirmed in place, so the ledger records no both-sides snapshot for this event — it records the gap itself, disclosed, together with the compensating evidence. At `integrity_events.deepseek_v4_ga_boundary`: `pre_launch_snapshot: null`, `disclosed_gap: true`, `alias_echo: "unchanged across the boundary — 231/231 July 2026 observations returned deepseek-v4-pro (data/model_versions/deepseek.jsonl)"`, and `behavioral_shift: "zero behavioral/failure/latency shift observed either side of the GA date (decision logs 2026-07-01..2026-07-27)"`. Both-sides capture was achieved for the May repoint; the V4-GA event registers what the apparatus does when a capture is missed — disclosure plus monitoring evidence, never silent continuity.

**Boundary documentation.** Every transition is recorded as a dated pair — config boundary date and first-logged-success date — in the integrity ledger, with the model-identity response snapshotted on both sides of each boundary.

**Segmentation.** DeepSeek's Phase A pilot series spans multiple model states. Verified against the ledger and `docs/MODEL_CHANGELOG.md`, the attested sequence is: the `deepseek-reasoner` alias from the 2026-04-08 initial baseline; `deepseek-v4-flash` (off-spec) from 2026-04-24; `deepseek-v4-pro` from the 2026-05-21 config boundary, first logged success 2026-05-22; and the provider's official V4 GA on 2026-07-20 *underneath an unchanged `deepseek-v4-pro` alias*. **The record attests the alias at launch, not the build serving it:** neither the ledger nor the changelog identifies which model answered under `deepseek-reasoner` before 2026-04-24, and the alias-echo capture records only the echoed alias string, so a build swap under an alias is unobservable by design. The shakedown state is therefore registered as *the `deepseek-reasoner` alias, underlying build unattested* — not as a named model version. Each state is a separate homogeneous window; no metric is pooled across a state boundary. A model change is a structural break, identical in treatment to a prompt-regime boundary.

**Rule instantiated.** *Pre-absorption:* provider-forced changes arriving during the pilot are absorbed in the pilot — the settled post-change version is what the confirmatory phase pins. *Detection:* because the provider's identifiers are non-dated aliases, identity is verified by capture and monitoring, not trusted from configuration (see §Snapshot-Integrity Limitations).

## T1.2 — Data-Integrity Framework (Registered Methodology)

### The three-gate inclusion framework

A model-month enters behavioral analysis only if it passes all three gates. Registered at `scripts/phase_a_integrity_ledger.json` → `inclusion_gates`, `completeness_min: 0.8`, `gates: ["completeness", "uncorrupted_book", "model_identity_stable"]`:

1. **Decision completeness ≥ 0.80** — ledger `gate_definitions.completeness`: *"decision_completeness (api_success / records over the month) >= completeness_min. COMPUTED per month from the decision logs (varies month to month)."*
2. **Uncorrupted book** — ledger `gate_definitions.uncorrupted_book`: *"the model's book is free of carried phantom cash / phantom shorts. CARRIED via persistent_model_status (a corrupt book is a state fact, not a monthly recompute)."* (See the conservation audit, §T1.3.)
3. **Stable model identity** — ledger `gate_definitions.model_identity_stable`: *"the deployed model identity did not silently change. CARRIED via persistent_model_status / integrity_events.deepseek_identity_splice."* Identity is verified by response capture where provider identifiers permit, by the compensating monitoring controls where they do not (§Snapshot-Integrity Limitations).

### The estimability taxonomy

Each model-month is classified by `scripts/build_monthly_data_layer.py::_evaluate_gates`, which emits a paired `data_confidence` / `profile_status` under a fixed gate priority — **book > identity > completeness**:

- **Estimable** — all three gates pass; eligible for all registered analyses. (`data_confidence: "estimable"`, `profile_status: "estimable_primary"`.)
- **Performance-only** — book integrity holds (equity and return series valid) but decision completeness fails; performance is reportable, behavioral metrics are not computed. (`data_confidence: "exploratory_completeness"`, `profile_status: "performance_only"`.)
- **Exploratory** — data usable only with disclosed caveats; never confirmatory. In the implementation this tier is reached by the identity gate: (`data_confidence: "exploratory_model_splice"`, `profile_status: "provisional_model_splice"`).
- **Non-estimable** — book corruption; excluded from all analysis. (`data_confidence: "non_estimable_corrupt_book"`, `profile_status: "non_estimable"`.) Phase A instance: the Claude Sonnet 4.6 and Claude Opus 4.6 books are non-estimable for all of Phase A due to launch-window corruption. The two events are registered at `integrity_events.launch_window_corruption.bugs`: `{"id": "launch_day_clobber", "occurred": "2026-04-09", "fix_date": "2026-04-10", "fix_commit": "d2e862ca", "models": "all_six"}` and `{"id": "cross_model_commingling", "occurred": "2026-04-10/2026-04-21", "fix_date": "2026-04-21", "fix_commit": "cacd8058", "models": ["claude", "claude_opus"]}` — note these are dated by occurrence and fix separately; April 10 and April 21 are the **fix** dates. The carried corruption is at `launch_window_corruption.persistence`: `carried_forward: {"claude": {"phantom_cash": 297000}, "claude_opus": {"phantom_shorts": 575000}}`, `scope: "phase_a_wide"`, `consequence: "non_estimable"`.

**Note on tier count.** The registered taxonomy above is four-tier by *label*; the implementation carries a fifth, orthogonal axis — `risk_basis` (`raw` / `raw_basis_sensitive` / `spliced` / `non_estimable`) — because the gates certify the point return, not the daily-risk path. The ledger states the open scope explicitly at `inclusion_gates.gate_scope_refinement`: *"book-integrity gate certifies point-return estimability, not daily-risk-path estimability when residual phantom sits in a volatile name; September pass."*

### The clean-window convention

The Phase A shakedown period is April 9–22, 2026 inclusive; the uniform pilot-analysis window begins April 23, 2026. The basis is evidence, not judgment: the last shakedown-class stabilization commit is `cacd8058` (April 21, 2026 — the state-file routing fix), with no stabilization activity April 22 – May 12; independently, the commingling corruption cleared ~April 22–23. Two repo-checkable signals bracket the same boundary; the pilot window is pinned at its conservative edge. Registered at ledger `clean_window_start: "2026-04-23"` and `shakedown_window: {"start": "2026-04-09", "end": "2026-04-22"}`.

### The integrity ledger

All dated boundaries — data-quality events, model-version boundaries, regime boundaries, corrections — are registered in a machine-readable integrity ledger (`scripts/phase_a_integrity_ledger.json`). **Verified against code:** `build_monthly_data_layer.py` takes the ledger as a build input and derives every boundary from it — `_evaluate_gates(model_keys, decision_completeness, clean_results, ledger)` reads `inclusion_gates` and `persistent_model_status`; `_conservation_audit_block(ledger, …)` reads `audit_certifications`; the month's incidents are lifted wholesale with `month_events = (ledger.get("operational_events") or {}).get(month_tag, [])` and emitted as `out["incidents"] = [dict(ev) for ev in month_events]`; and completeness segmentation is derived from each event's own `effective` field rather than a literal. The ledger's own `_README` states the rule: *"Per-month-derivable facts (completeness gate, clean-window returns, the passing cohort) are COMPUTED by the builder, never stored here."* The one prior violation of ledger-as-source — `report_meta.regime` and `prompt_version` hard-coded to `"v1"` — was fixed in commit `d308a607`, after which both are derived from the decision logs' `prompt_version` stamps.

The reporting rule is directional: **prose corrects to the committed data layer, never the reverse.** Where a narrative claim and the layer conflict, the layer wins and the narrative is corrected — the mechanism behind the published erratum, registered at `reports/monthly/2026-05/data_layer.json` → `report_meta.provenance.spy_anchor_correction` (dated 2026-07-01), which records `from: {"inception_value": 679.9099731445312, "cumulative_return_since_inception": 0.11261786170026067}`, `to: {"inception_value": 680.4000244140625, "cumulative_return_since_inception": 0.11181650988358638}`, and closes: *"Published May prose citing the old 11.26 percent SPY figure requires a Reports erratum (PDF not edited)."* The re-rendered artifact is `reports/monthly/2026-05/May_2026_Monthly_Report_rev2.pdf`.

## T1.3 — Conservation Audit

**Design.** Every recorded fill is replayed through the real portfolio-accounting engine — not a parallel simplified model — and the resulting state is checked for equity continuity. **Verified against code** (`scripts/build_monthly_data_layer.py::_conservation_audit_block`), the implemented invariant is singular, not a set: each fill is replayed through the real `Portfolio` under a **constant-price mark**, with the traded name held at its own fill price, *"the only thing that can move equity at this step is the fill"*; the audit then asserts equity continuity across the step, `disc = p.total_value(marks) - equity_before`, failing when `abs(disc) > _AUDIT_TOL` with `_AUDIT_TOL = 1e-6`. Two further behaviors complete it: a **replay-infeasible** failure mode, which records `f"{desc}: replay infeasible ({e})"` when the fill cannot be applied to the book at all; and a **sweep-dust epsilon allowance**, which tolerates a discontinuity bounded by `eps * price + _AUDIT_TOL` where a fill sweeps a sub-epsilon share residual (`GHOST_SHARES_EPSILON` dust — *"a 4-dp-rounded sell leaving 0.0001 shares"*). **Cash conservation and position/share conservation are implied by this construction — they are not separately asserted.** Under a constant-price mark, any failure to conserve cash or shares surfaces as an equity discontinuity; the audit tests the consequence, not each component.

The audit is self-running at build time: the monthly builder certifies its own month before the report can build. **Verified against code**, the build sequence in `build()` is `_decision_completeness(layer)` → `_evaluate_gates(…)` → `_conservation_audit_block(…)` → `_apply_integrity(…)`, with the audit interposed before the integrity layer is applied and the layer returned. Any in-month violation raises before anything is emitted: `RuntimeError(f"CONSERVATION AUDIT HALT: {len(violations)} conservation violation(s) in {month} — do not write a clean certification over a dirty finding: …")`.

**Certification registry.** Each audited month is recorded in a per-month certification registry at `scripts/phase_a_integrity_ledger.json` → `integrity_events.launch_window_corruption.audit_certifications`, keyed by report month — currently `2026-05`, `2026-06`, `2026-07` — giving each published report a machine-checkable audit provenance. The ledger's `_README` states the rhythm: *"when a computed month's build is accepted, append its emitted audit block here under its month key."*

**Violation semantics.** A conservation violation does not auto-correct: it **halts** the build and opens an investigation; the resolution (correction + erratum, or reclassification of the affected model-month under the estimability taxonomy) is itself a ledger event. Silent repair is prohibited by design — the phantom-cash/phantom-short events (§T1.2) are classified, not patched.

**Record.** As of this draft, the certification registry states, for the latest certified month (`audit_certifications["2026-07"]`): `runs: "6000+"`, `unit: "per_model_runs"`, `anomalies: 0`, over `clean_runs: {"clean_april": 73, "may": 257, "june": 270, "july": 283}` from `clean_after: "2026-04-23"`. **6,000+ per-model runs certified, zero anomalies.** The registry does not record a fill count, and the sweep-dust tolerance is a property of the audit code (§T1.3 Design), not a registry entry; the certification claim states only what the registry states.

## T1.4 — Cap Equity: A Methods Worked Example

**The defect class.** Setting an identical nominal parameter across providers does not produce identical effective constraints, because providers account tokens differently. Concretely: for providers whose thinking/reasoning tokens draw from the same budget as the answer (Google; DeepSeek; the Anthropic 5-family), a nominal `max_output_tokens = 4096` leaves the *answer* budget equal to 4096 minus whatever thinking consumed — while a provider with separate accounting delivers the full 4096 to the answer. Nominal equality, effective inequality.

**Origin.** The principle was first implemented in the DeepSeek arm: the 2026-05-21 v4-pro adapter set the equalized budget with an in-code rationale — the earliest implementation of effective-budget equality. From `src/adapters/deepseek_adapter.py`: *"max_tokens is the shared budget for the reasoning trace + the final JSON answer, raised from 4096 to 16384 so a long high-effort trace can't truncate the decision (v4-pro supports far more, and reasoning tokens are billed regardless of the cap, so the headroom is free on short replies)."* The Gemini fix then propagated it.

**The instance.** The Gemini arm at a 4096 budget produced systematically truncated decisions — the thinking share consumed the budget and answers terminated mid-generation — while peers at the same nominal setting were unconstrained. Registered at `operational_events["2026-07"].gemini_budget_equalization`, which records the ruling and the mechanism: *"constraint identity is defined at effective decision budget, per provider, not nominal parameter equality. gemini-3.1-pro-preview's hidden thinking spends against the shared output budget, so at 4096 the visible JSON decision truncated mid-string at ~150 tokens with finish_reason=MAX_TOKENS — the mechanism behind the May 0.57 / June 0.79 / July 0.51 completeness failures."* Raising the budget to 16384 eliminated the truncation mode entirely: the confirmation entry at `gemini_budget_equalization.in_situ_verification` records `max_tokens_rate: 0.0` and `halt_condition: "not triggered (threshold >1% MAX_TOKENS on any day; observed 0/39 cycles)"` over the first three live trading days at the raised budget. It did not restore 100% conversion — the same block records the residual: *"2026-07-29 had 2/13 failures, both finish_reason=STOP JSON parse errors (trailing 'Extra data' after the decision object) — the pre-existing, budget-independent failure mode."*

**The registered principle.** Constraint equality is defined at the **effective decision budget** — the resources actually available to produce the decision — not at the nominal parameter value. Where provider accounting differs, nominal parameters are set per-provider so that effective budgets are equal, and the per-provider settings are documented in the per-model API-configuration table. The principle is applied prospectively: any model added or refreshed with shared thinking/answer accounting enters at the equalized budget (16384), not the nominal default `[SEPTEMBER: the 5-family thinking-parity configuration, ratified with the cohort rule]`.

**Why registered as methods.** "Identical constraints" is load-bearing for every cross-model claim in this study. This example documents that the project's definition of identical is effective, not nominal — and that the one detected violation of effective equality was found, fixed, equalized, and disclosed.

## T1.5 — Benchmark and Anchor

**The benchmark.** Passive SPY, anchored at the deployment moment: the inception anchor is **680.40**, captured April 9, 2026 and pinned in the integrity ledger at `spy_benchmark_anchor` — `{"date": "2026-04-09", "benchmark_value": 680.4000244140625, "field": "benchmark_value"}`, sourced as *"First real SPY capture at capital deployment — the 2026-04-09 first post-deployment tick (the day's total_value moves off the $100k seed at this row)."* All benchmark-relative figures across all surfaces (reports, dashboard, paper) compute from this single pinned anchor; the same ledger note records the unification: *"All four SPY surfaces (perf-table alpha, leaderboard row, dashboard equity curve, monthly `_spy_eod_series`) read this pin."* The unification landed in commit `87abe434` (*"spy: deterministic canonical series + ledger-pinned inception anchor"*); the May re-audit is the `spy_anchor_correction` provenance block cited in §T1.2.

**The determinism defect (found and fixed).** **Verified against the ledger and the committed layer.** The defect record is `spy_benchmark_anchor.note`: *"The 2026-04-09 perf rows carry THREE conflicting benchmark_value observations (676.01 pre-deployment seed = the 2026-04-08 shakedown value; 680.40 first real tick; 679.91 later tick), and pre-inception 2026-04-08 rows @ 676.01 also exist. `groupby(date).last()` over the unstably-ordered multi-model concat resolved to 676.01 or 679.91 depending on row ordering (10.32% / 9.69% for inception->2026-07-01), a non-deterministic anchor."* The defect was not a live re-query against a vendor: it was **non-deterministic row selection within the committed series** — the same inputs yielded different anchors depending on concat ordering. The fix pinned the anchor: *"Pinned to 680.40 so the inception anchor is deterministic: inception->2026-07-01 = 9.61%"* (commit `87abe434`), with `resolves: "SPY inception-anchor non-determinism (dated 2026-07-01; leaderboard 10.32% / alpha 9.69% / dashboard 10.32% for one fixed window)"` — three surfaces disagreeing on one fixed window. The erratum linkage is the May layer's `report_meta.provenance.spy_anchor_correction`, moving SPY cumulative-since-inception from `0.11261786170026067` to `0.11181650988358638` — a correction of **8.0 bp** — re-rendered as `May_2026_Monthly_Report_rev2.pdf`. The corrected convention is registered below because the defect demonstrated why it is needed.

**The registered convention.** The **persisted series is canonical**: benchmark and price series are captured once, committed to the versioned data layer, and all published figures compute from the committed series — never from a live re-query at build or read time. Published monthly layers are **frozen at month-end**: a published month's data layer is immutable, insulating published reports from upstream data-vendor revisions; any post-publication correction is an explicit erratum with a ledger entry, never a silent regeneration. **Reproducibility claim (layer-level).** Rebuilding any published month's data layer from raw inputs plus the integrity ledger reproduces the committed layer, modulo documented build-time fields; the claim is regression-enforced (the reproduce-May test is the standing proof). **Verified against code** (`tests/test_reproduce_may_data_layer.py`): the comparison is a strict recursive structural diff over the whole layer — `test_reproduce_may_data_layer_exact` asserts zero diffs — with exactly three documented exclusions, `EXCLUDE_EXACT = {".report_meta.generated_at_utc", ".report_meta.source_commit"}` and `EXCLUDE_PREFIX = (".report_meta.provenance",)`, commented in source as *"Fields legitimately not reproducible by a pre-commit build."* A second test, `test_reproduce_may_passes_definition_of_done`, enforces the backfill's own verification pass. The rendered report (PDF) is a Design artifact and is **not** byte-reproducible; the registered claim is the layer, not the rendering.
